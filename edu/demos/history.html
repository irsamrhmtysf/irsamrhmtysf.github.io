Three Paradigms of AI

How the dominant approach to AI has shifted over time. Each wave built on — and sometimes replaced — the last.

1950s – 1980s

Symbolic AI

Hand-coded rules and logic. Expert systems. IF-THEN reasoning. Could solve constrained problems but couldn't handle uncertainty or learn.

~30% of current AI research
1980s – 2010s

Statistical AI

Learning patterns from data using probability. SVMs, Naive Bayes, decision trees. Requires feature engineering by humans.

~20% of current AI research
2012 – present

Deep Learning

Neural networks learn hierarchical representations directly from raw data. No manual feature engineering. Requires large data and compute.

~85% of current AI research

Narrow AI (ANI) — exists today

Superhuman performance on one specific task. Cannot transfer that skill to another domain. Examples: AlphaGo (Go only), GPT-4 (language), AlphaFold (protein structure).

General AI (AGI) — not yet achieved

Human-level performance across any cognitive task, including learning new domains. Expert timelines range from 2027 to "never". Currently the field's central open question.
